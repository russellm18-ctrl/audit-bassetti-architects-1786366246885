## Atmospheric Reality Gap

No hero images are attached to this message, so this audit cannot render a judgment on the photography's quality, light, or composition — those are visual calls that require visual evidence. What the forensic scan does reveal is a structural mismatch worth naming.

Your homepage copy leads with "Fostering Healthy Communities through Learning and Design" — a purpose-driven, community-forward promise. That's the contract you're offering every visitor. The image infrastructure meant to prove that promise includes filenames like `B002002000_hmchigh.jpg`, `B001914000_N30_hmcfull.jpg`, and `Cover+images4.jpg` — internal job-code strings that communicate nothing to the humans or search systems processing them. When the filename carries zero semantic weight, the image does all the lifting alone, with no backup from the asset layer. If the photography is strong, this is a waste. If it ever rotates or degrades in quality, there is no naming system to enforce the standard. Either way, the copy's emotional promise is being supported by infrastructure built for a file server, not a client's trust.

The trust cost: a buyer who arrives already primed by your #1 Google ranking and then encounters a gap between the warmth of your mission language and the clinical opacity of your image assets is quietly recalibrating their confidence in your attention to detail — before they've clicked a single project.

## Visual Narrative Coherence

The most expensive fracture in your buyer journey is a dead portfolio link sitting inside your homepage image carousel. The thumbnail for `1915_N13_large.jpg` links directly to `https://bassettiarch.com/portfoliodetails/wallawalla` — confirmed 404. A high-value institutional client (school board, healthcare system, municipal agency) comparing three shortlisted firms clicks that image and lands on an error page. That client does not file a bug report. They move to the next tab.

Two additional broken links compound this signal: your LinkedIn company page returns a 429 error and your Facebook profile returns a 400. Both are surfaced in the homepage header and footer navigation. For a firm whose work is inherently relational and community-focused, dead social links don't read as a technical glitch — they read as organizational disengagement. That's a credibility fracture at the exact moment a buyer is trying to verify your firm's active presence before recommending you to a selection committee.

Three broken links out of 25 checked is a 12% failure rate on your homepage link infrastructure. That's not a rounding error — it's a pattern a discerning buyer notices.

## Metadata and Conversion Gaps

Your page title is "Bassetti Architects" — 19 characters. Google allows roughly 60. You are leaving 41 characters of free, indexed positioning on the table. A competitor bidding on "educational architecture Pacific Northwest" or "community-centered school design" gets that space; you do not. Your meta description clocks in at 58 characters — a fragment, not a pitch. Every Google impression your #1 ranking earns is followed by a description that fails to close the argument for the click.

Your 30 homepage content images have 0% missing alt text, which is the one clean number in this audit — no images are invisible to screen readers or search crawlers. The problem is that alt text written against filenames like `16999_N36_large.jpg` and `e_BensonTech_5_lowres.jpg` is almost certainly pulling from those same opaque naming conventions, which means the accessibility win may be technical compliance without genuine descriptive value. The scan cannot confirm alt text content, only coverage — but the filename pattern predicts the outcome.

You rank #1 for your own brand name. That's baseline, not a moat. The metadata gap means you're invisible to the institutional buyers who don't yet know to search for you by name.
